# Demo content and directions

## Content before form

The audience is a general web reader exploring a product or technical explanation. A five-part optical assembly tests a simple sequence: recognize the whole, select parts, reveal structure, and understand assembly order along one shared axis. A barrel, biconvex lenses, hollow spacer, and retainer have distinct structural reasons for their forms. Interchangeable boxes would not express them. Dimensions and curvature are conceptual, without claims about optical performance.

The examples are standalone pages with no host design system. Quiet pale backgrounds, muted metals, and system fonts support comparison here; they are neither plugin defaults nor a user-approved final language.

## Community observations

- [model-viewer dimensions example](https://modelviewer.dev/examples/annotations/#dimensions): documentation was read and the armchair and Show Dimensions control were inspected in a browser. Materials and silhouette identify the subject; the information layer can be toggled. Some dimension text was clipped at the observed viewport edge. The useful idea is controllable information, with independent checks for occlusion and clipping. No model, texture, screenshot, or code was copied, and model-viewer is not a dependency.
- [Codrops: Exploding 3D Objects with Three.js](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2019/03/26/exploding-3d-objects-with-three-js/) and [Transparent Glass and Plastic](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2021/10/27/creating-the-effect-of-transparent-glass-and-plastic-in-three-js/): discovered during community research; their live visual quality was not verified. Fragmentation is unsuitable for this assembly relationship. Transmission rendering may inform a future glass treatment; the present lenses use opaque schematic materials for structural clarity.

These observations supplemented and checked the candidate directions. Research performed after implementation is not represented as prior research. The full cycle of user direction selection followed by several refinement rounds has not been completed in this demonstration.

## Two directions to compare

| Direction | Main value | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A: Section study | Immediate interior reading; orthographic framing and a manual slider support gradual explanation | A half-shell cannot establish complete exterior appearance; full expansion on narrow screens needs focus for detail |
| B: Product study | Complete silhouette and materials first, then active expansion; diagonal narrow-screen framing preserves subject size | Interior relationships require an action; greater camera freedom needs a deliberate teaching path |

Prefer A for assembly explanation and B for product presentation, or refine either. Independent subagents built the studies with the same parts and accuracy constraints. The lead agent inspected images and reran real-input checks before assembling the [comparison page](../examples/directions/index.html). Both remain candidates, without an assumed user preference.

The comparison primarily concerns reading and operation. Aesthetic language remains similar: pale backgrounds, muted metals, and fine-line UI. These are not two fully developed aesthetic alternatives. A dedicated aesthetic comparison would keep content and core tasks consistent while exploring composition, viewing distance, lighting, and material treatment further.
